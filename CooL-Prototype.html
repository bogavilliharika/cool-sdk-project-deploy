Threat model

What CooL protects against — and what it does not prove.

✓ Modification of stored evidence

A changed recorded field produces a different commitment, so integrity mismatch can be detected.

✓ Forged execution records

A receipt can be checked against its commitment; stronger authenticity requires the unimplemented signature layer.

✓ Misattributed software identity

Software/model identity is part of the committed evidence fields, but cryptographic identity attestation is not implemented in this prototype.

△ Deletion or loss of evidence

A commitment detects alteration but cannot by itself prove a deleted record ever existed. Durable external anchoring is needed.

△ Compromised logging infrastructure

The prototype demonstrates integrity checks, but real signatures, external transparency witnesses and hardware attestation are required for stronger independent trust.

✕ Not proved by CooL

CooL verifies evidence integrity and provenance. It does not prove an AI decision was correct, fair, safe, or appropriate.

Prototype honesty note
Implemented now: browser SHA-256 commitment generation/recomputation, tamper comparison, receipt export/import, and local evidence metadata viewer.

Demo / not implemented: Ed25519 verification, ML-DSA-65 verification, RFC 6962 inclusion/consistency verification, and TEE attestation verification.
